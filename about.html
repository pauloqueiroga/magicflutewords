Magic Flute Words

Last updated: October 25, 2026

Not to be confused with The Magic Flute, Mozart's 1791 opera.

Magic Flute Words is a free browser-based word game in which players guess five hidden five-letter words each day. Every puzzle opens with the same three clue words, MAGIC, FLUTE and WORDS, which together reveal 15 letters of the alphabet before the player makes a single guess. The game takes its name from those three words.

The game uses the three-way letter feedback popularized by Wordle: each letter of a guess is marked as correct and in place, present but misplaced, or absent. While Wordle asks for one word a day and leaves the opening guesses to the player, Magic Flute Words asks for five and supplies the opening itself. Each solved word earns up to five stars, for a daily maximum of 25.

Magic Flute Words launched on March 9, 2026. It is developed by Paulo Queiroga and hosted on GitHub Pages. It requires no account, shows no advertising, and is funded by voluntary donations through Ko-fi.

Gameplay

Each day brings five hidden five-letter words, which the player solves one at a time. The puzzle changes at midnight Coordinated Universal Time, so every player worldwide gets the same five words on the same day. Guesses must be valid five-letter words.

Clue words

MAGIC, FLUTE and WORDS are shown as free starter clues for every hidden word. The three words share no letters, so between them they test 15 different ones: A, C, D, E, F, G, I, L, M, O, R, S, T, U and W. All five of the vowels A, E, I, O and U are among them.

The 11 letters the clues leave untested are B, H, J, K, N, P, Q, V, X, Y and Z. Finding which of these belong in the hidden word is left to the player's own guesses.

Feedback

After each guess, every letter is marked in one of three ways: correct letter in the correct position, letter in the word but in the wrong position, or letter not in the word. Guesses can be typed on a physical keyboard or the on-screen one.

Scoring

Players earn up to five stars per word. A word solved on the first guess earns five stars, and each extra guess costs one star. There is no limit on guesses: a word that takes six or more earns no stars but stays in play until it is solved. Five first-try solves make a perfect day of 25 stars.

Other features

  • Statistics: games played, stars earned, performance and the distribution of stars per word.
  • Archive: every past game is numbered and can be played from the game number at the top of the screen.
  • Sharing: a finished game can be shared or copied as a short summary of the game number, date, stars earned and a link.
  • Definitions: a magnifying-glass button looks up a word's meaning through Google Search.
  • Transfer: saved progress can be moved to another device through a transfer link.

Comparison to Wordle

Wordle is among the most prominent word puzzle video games; in 2022 it topped Google's worldwide Year in Search list. Magic Flute Words builds on the idea of five-letter words, three-way feedback and one shared puzzle a day, but changes how much of the puzzle the player has to discover.

Because the clue words never change, there is no choice of starting word, a long-running topic of Wordle strategy. Play begins with the information a Wordle player would gather from three well-chosen opening guesses, and the challenge shifts to the 11 untested letters and to placing the letters already found.

The five-word format also recalls Quordle, which asks for four words a day. Quordle runs its four boards at the same time; Magic Flute Words presents its words one at a time and scores each separately.

Development and release

Magic Flute Words is an independent project by Paulo Queiroga, published as a static website from a public GitHub repository and served through GitHub Pages at magicflutewords.com. The game runs entirely in the browser, with nothing to download and no sign-up.

The first daily puzzle, Game #1, was published on March 9, 2026. In its early months the game was played mostly by the developer's friends and family.

The game was first hosted at a different address before moving to its current domain. The site includes a link-based import that carries players' saved games over from the old address.

The site ships a web app manifest, so players can add it to a phone's home screen, where it opens in portrait in its own window, without the browser's address bar, like a native app. Each browser and each home-screen copy keeps its own saved games.

Funding and privacy

The game is free and serves no advertising. Players who want to support it can donate through a Ko-fi button on the site.

The game never asks for a name, email address or other personal details. Stars and unfinished guesses are kept in the browser's local storage rather than on a server. Transfer links carry saved games inside the link itself, and the game strips that data from the address bar as soon as the page loads.

Visits are counted with Google Analytics, with Google's advertising features switched off for all players. Visitors whose device time zone is in Europe are asked for consent before analytics loads, and any player can turn it off under Cookie settings. The privacy policy has the details.
